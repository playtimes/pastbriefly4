# Film #3 Evidence Brief

This is a selection brief, **not a selection**. No story is chosen here.

**Evidence base:**
- `CHANNEL-BASELINE.md` and `VIDEO-AUDIT.md`: 11 PastBriefly Shorts (26-49 s) and 7,104 views.
- Official YouTube Analytics API for 2026-08-01 to 2026-09-26 (primary).
- Reporting API for 2026-08-27 to 2026-09-25 (enrichment only).
- vidIQ as a secondary cross-check.
- **There is no PastBriefly long-form data.**

Labels:
- **STRONG SIGNAL:** multiple independent videos.
- **WEAK SIGNAL:** little data.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** to test.
- **UNKNOWN:** the data cannot answer it.

## Strongest channel evidence

| Finding | Label |
|---|---|
| Distribution comes from the Shorts feed: 93.0% of channel views, and 97.4% of Tree's launch views. Search, browse and external traffic are marginal. | STRONG SIGNAL |
| A Short's distribution happens in its first 1-2 days, then stops (Tree 99.1%, Tupac 91.6% in 2 days). The channel is near-dormant between uploads. | STRONG SIGNAL |
| The four Shorts with the weakest early hold (below 0.65 AWR at 10 s) all had 300 views or fewer. Every other Short was at 0.80 or higher at 10 s. | STRONG SIGNAL (association, direction of cause unknown) |
| The fastest early loss is in the 4-7 s window for 10 of 11 Shorts. | STRONG SIGNAL (descriptive) |
| Three Shorts show replay behaviour (APV 105-113%, end AWR 0.84-1.01). | STRONG SIGNAL (descriptive) |
| Good retention did not guarantee distribution (Eclipse 123 views, Dead man 549, Rat poison 577). | WEAK SIGNAL |
| Thumbnail impressions / CTR from Reporting do not measure the Shorts feed, and cover the launch of only 2 of 11 Shorts. | STRONG SIGNAL (descriptive) for the first part. Launch reach/CTR is UNKNOWN for 9 Shorts. |
| Subscriber conversion differences between stories. | UNKNOWN (0-3 subscribers per Short) |

## What appears to win the feed gate

- The four feed winners (1,172-1,422 views, 555-661 engaged views):
  - Warship that sank after 20 minutes
  - Pig that nearly started a war
  - 813 troops sent to cut down one tree
  - Passenger jet that ran out of fuel
- The current sample provides evidence that **immediately understandable concrete premises** (one visible object or animal plus a physical event, or a trivial cause plus an outsized response) **were more likely to receive strong feed distribution.** **WEAK SIGNAL.**
  - It holds for 4 of 4 winners.
  - But Rat poison and Dead man are also concrete and reached only 549-577 views.
  - Each shape has only 2 examples.
- Clearing the watch gate (section below) went with every feed win. But three Shorts cleared it without winning the feed. **WEAK SIGNAL.**
- A legal-process premise with a famous name (Tupac) drew search traffic (36.5% of its views) rather than feed distribution. **WEAK SIGNAL** (1 video).
- **UNKNOWN:** feed "shown vs swiped away", and launch impressions for 9 of 11 Shorts.

## What appears to win the watch gate

- **The level of AWR at 10 s** is the clearest dividing line in the data (all 11 Shorts sort into two groups):
  - Seven Shorts are at 0.80-1.39 at 10 s: Jet, Rat poison, Dead man, Tree, Eclipse, Pig, Warship.
  - Four are at 0.43-0.64: Tupac, 1816, Lake Nyos, Trader.
  - Label: STRONG SIGNAL (association).
- The 4->7 s drop size alone does not separate them (Jet 0.23 against 1816 0.24). The curves differ in how high they start, which reflects replays, and in total loss by 10 s.
- **Strongest holds:**
  - Jet stays above 1.0 AWR for the whole video.
  - Rat poison and Dead man are above 0.95 at 50%.
  - Tree has the best non-looping midpoint (0.76 AWR, and a relative 0.59 tied best).
- **Weakest holds:** Trader, 1816, Tupac and Lake Nyos (APV 36-50%).

## Story characteristics worth testing

These are HYPOTHESES for Film #3 selection, drawn from Short-era evidence. None is a rule.

1. **The premise can be stated in one sentence with no background needed.** The strangeness is clear from the sentence alone. (Supported by the 4 feed winners, and by the weak views and retention of Trader and Tupac: WEAK SIGNAL.)
2. **A central physical object, animal or vehicle, with a concrete physical event that can be shown.** (4 winners, but also 2 concrete mid-performers: WEAK SIGNAL.)
3. **Built-in stakes or a paradox that rewards a rewatch.** All three loopers (lives at risk in the air, a poisoned marathon runner, a corpse deceiving an army) have a detail that is hard to believe on first hearing. (HYPOTHESIS: 3 videos, and looping did not by itself produce distribution.)
4. **Untested, not disproven:**
   - Natural-disaster stories (confounded with label titles).
   - Science stories (Eclipse held well but was not distributed).
   - Institutional or financial stories.
5. **Not supported by this data:**
   - "military always wins"
   - "physical objects always win"
   - any runtime being optimal (the loopers are 26, 39 and 49 s)
   - any title formula being objectively best
   - deception stories converting subscribers (Dead man: 0)

## Opening requirements

The official retention curves show that the first 10 s decide much of each Short's retention. For Film #3 these become three **opening goals**, each labelled by how much the data supports it. None is a guarantee of distribution, and with 11 Shorts none is statistically significant.

For the two supporting Shorts (checked in the 7-day Analytics snapshot):

1. **HISTORICAL BENCHMARK: AWR at 10 s of 0.75 or more.** The strongest useful benchmark in this sample. The lowest of the seven Shorts that held was 0.80 (Warship), and the highest of the four that did not was 0.64 (Tupac).
2. **EXPERIMENTAL TARGET: AWR of 0.80 or more around 25% of the Short.** A hypothesis to test, not a proven threshold. Every Short over 1,100 views reached 0.81 or more here, and every Short below 0.65 at 10 s was at 0.61 or less.
3. **CREATIVE HYPOTHESIS: the strange premise is understandable before about 4 s.** Informed by the concrete-premise Shorts that won the feed, not proven by the retention data. Check it on the edit before publishing.

For the Long there is no benchmark. Record the retention at 5 s, 10 s and 30 s and compare it with the Shorts' curves, but set no target until one Long exists.

## Short strategy

The two supporting Shorts should each test one variable. Keep the story identical, so any difference points to the opening rather than the topic.

1. **Short A: premise-first.** The complete concrete premise lands in the first sentence, under 4 s.
2. **Short B: same story, different entry point.** Open on the stakes, a number or the paradox, and put the full premise after it.
3. **Compare A and B on:**
   - views and engaged views at 48 h and 7 days (feed gate)
   - AWR at 10 s, AWR at 25% and 50%, end AWR, and APV (watch gate)
   - RRP at 50%, as a relative check only
4. **Both Shorts:** use the related-video link to the Long, and measure Short -> Long views from the Long's traffic sources. The Reporting traffic report records the source per video per day. Nothing like this has been measured before.
5. **Baselines to beat are channel medians over 11 Shorts:**
   - 549 views
   - 178 engaged views
   - APV 70.35%
   - AWR at 50% of 0.65

## Film #3 measurement plan

**Tools:**
- The local read-only commands already built: `npm run youtube:analytics -- --start <publish date> --end <window end> --videos <Long,ShortA,ShortB>` and `npm run youtube:reporting:sync`.

Record everything in one small table per window, so Film #4 can be compared like for like.
